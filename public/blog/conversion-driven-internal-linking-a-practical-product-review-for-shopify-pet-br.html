Real-world examples: conversion paths for pet brands on Shopify

Let’s get practical. Conversion-driven internal linking on Shopify pet brands should do three jobs every time someone lands on your site.

  • Answer a pet question, then give one clear next click to a relevant collection.
  • Support product discovery, then route visitors to comparison or category pages that match their intent.
  • Reduce dead ends, so every post has a path that respects the buying journey.

Here is a simple linking flow we often build for ecommerce internal linking ecommerce setups:

  1. A top-of-funnel pet guide post introduces the topic (example intent: diet and ingredients).
  2. Inside the guide, we place contextual links to the most relevant food or treat collection.
  3. On the collection page, we link back to deeper supporting posts (example intent: ingredient breakdowns, feeding guidance).
  4. Every new post gets stitched into the same structure so orphaned content does not accumulate.

Conversion-Driven Internal Linking vs. “linking for the sake of linking”

This is the part we are blunt about, because most teams never fix it. If your internal linking is random, it does not scale and it does not convert.

Linking for sake of linking

  • Links go to whatever page is convenient, not the most relevant collection.
  • Anchor text is vague, so the next click is not tied to buyer intent.
  • Old posts never get reworked, so they stop supporting conversion journeys.

Conversion-driven internal linking

  • Links go to the pages that match the visitor’s intent and next action.
  • Anchor text aligns with the buying question (like product type and needs).
  • Every update compounds, because your internal linking architecture is maintained.
